Understand

A short history, with the disputes marked.

Dates and figures come from the sources linked; where sources disagree, the box says so.

1917–1948
British rule (the Mandate) follows the Ottoman Empire. In 1947 the UN proposes partitioning Palestine.
1948
Israel declares independence and a war follows. About 750,000 Palestinians flee or are expelled, an event Palestinians call the Nakba. Historians debate the causes.
1967
Israel takes the West Bank, East Jerusalem, and Gaza. The occupation continues.
1993
The Oslo Accords create the Palestinian Authority.
2005–2007
Israel withdraws its settlers and troops from Gaza. Hamas takes control of the Strip in 2007, and Israel and Egypt restrict movement in and out.
7 October 2023
Hamas-led attackers kill about 1,200 people in Israel and take about 250 hostage (Israeli figures). Israel launches a war in Gaza.
Since 7 October 2023
Gaza’s health ministry reports more than 73,000 people killed and more than 173,000 injured (73,246 and 173,727 as of 24 July 2026, relayed by the UN Health Cluster).
September 2025
A UN Commission of Inquiry finds that Israel has committed genocide in Gaza. Israel rejects the finding. South Africa’s case at the ICJ is still open.
10 October 2025
A ceasefire begins, with a hostage exchange and a surge in aid. Violence continues at lower intensity.
2026
Reconstruction materials stay restricted, and settler violence in the West Bank is high.

What’s contested

  • Casualty counts come from Gaza’s health ministry, which operates under the de facto authorities. The UN relays them with that caveat; the Israeli government has questioned them.
  • Whether Israel’s conduct is genocide: a UN Commission of Inquiry says yes, Israel rejects it, and the ICJ has not ruled; the case is still in its written phase.
  • Who is violating the ceasefire: Israel and Hamas accuse each other of violations.
  • UNRWA: Israel accuses it of being infiltrated by Hamas and has moved against its operations, including demolishing its East Jerusalem offices in January 2026. UNRWA denies tolerating or collaborating with Hamas and says it acted against staff accused of taking part in the attack (PBS News).
  • Aid restrictions: UN agencies say limits on “dual-use” items delay reconstruction; Israel cites security concerns.
