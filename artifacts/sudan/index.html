Sudan

Free ways to help, and the context behind them.

Give Listen Follow

Where things stand. The war between the army (SAF) and the Rapid Support Forces (RSF) began on 15 April 2023. The army retook Khartoum in 2025; the RSF took El Fasher in October 2025. Drones now harm civilians on both sides: in August 2026 the RSF struck Khartoum, El Obeid, and other army-held cities, and on 3 September UN investigators said foreign recruitment and weapons transfers are fuelling the war and found reasonable grounds to believe army drones have harmed civilians too. The UN estimated 13.6 million people uprooted in January 2026. Checked 21 September 2026. Every link goes to its own source. Download as PDF

Understand

A short history, with the disputes marked.

Dates and figures come from the sources linked; where sources disagree, the box says so.

1956
Independence from Britain and Egypt. Civil wars follow (1955–72, 1983–2005); South Sudan becomes independent in 2011.
1989
Omar al-Bashir takes power in a coup.
2003
Fighting in Darfur; government-backed Janjaweed militias, which later become the RSF. In October 2025 the ICC convicted a former Janjaweed leader of war crimes and crimes against humanity (HRW).
2018–2021
A popular uprising ousts Bashir in 2019 and a power-sharing deal follows. A military takeover ends it in October 2021.
April 2023
The army under Abdel Fattah al-Burhan and the RSF under Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo (Hemedti) begin fighting in Khartoum.
2025
The army retakes Khartoum. In January the US State Department determines the RSF committed genocide. By the end of October El Fasher falls to the RSF, with reports of killings of people fleeing (HRW).
2026
UN investigators say the RSF’s conduct in El Fasher shows hallmarks of genocide (February). Drones now harm civilians on both sides: the RSF struck Khartoum, El Obeid, and other cities in August, and the UN found reasonable grounds to believe army drones have harmed civilians too (September). The Security Council weighs renewing sanctions in September.

What’s contested

  • Both sides are accused of atrocities and of blocking aid. The UN fact-finding mission concluded that the RSF and allied forces used starvation as a method of warfare, a war crime (HRW).
  • UN investigators reported on 3 September 2026 that transnational networks recruit and deploy foreign personnel and move weapons to support the RSF, and urged extending the arms embargo to all of Sudan (UN News). They also found reasonable grounds to believe the army used foreign-supplied drones that harmed civilians (OHCHR).
  • Sudan now has rival authorities: an army-aligned government and an RSF-led parallel government.
